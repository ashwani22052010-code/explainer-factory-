What if you were born in the Wild West? Kansas, eighteen seventy. You are born in a small wooden house at the edge of a cattle town. Your father is the town blacksmith. Every morning you wake up to the sound of his hammer. Your mother runs a tiny bakery, and the whole street smells of fresh bread. Life is simple, but it is never easy. When you turn twelve, a fever sweeps through the town. Your father does not survive it. Now you are the man of the house. Two years later, you do something brave. You join a cattle drive. Three thousand cows, eleven cowboys, and one long trail north to the railroad. You are the youngest rider. They call you Kid. The work is brutal. Sixteen hours in the saddle. Dust in your eyes, rain on your back. One night, thunder spooks the herd. The ground shakes. A stampede. You ride through the dark, heart pounding, until the cattle finally calm down. The trail boss looks at you and nods. You did good, Kid. Weeks later, you reach the river. It is wide, fast and cold. The cows swim, the horses swim, and you hold on for your life. On the other side, you can finally see it. The railroad town. Money, music, saloons, and trains going east. You get paid more money than you have ever seen. And for the first time, you send some home to your mother. Years pass. The railroads grow. Fences cut across the open land. By eighteen ninety, the government declares the frontier closed. The age of the cowboy is ending. You buy a small piece of land, and you start a ranch of your own. And every evening, you sit on the porch, watching the sunset over the land you once rode across, as a kid with nothing.
